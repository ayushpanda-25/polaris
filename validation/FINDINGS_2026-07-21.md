# Skylit vs Polaris — GEX comparison findings (2026-07-21 session)

One full trading day, SPY + QQQ, 96 logged snapshots (09:31–15:31 ET). Skylit is ~live;
Polaris is CBOE ~15-min delayed, so every "valid pair" below is Skylit at T matched to a
Polaris capture describing the same market second (Polaris stamps CST = ET−1h). Timing is
ruled out — several pairs are same-second.

## Headline: the two maps disagree on the SIGN of the dominant near-spot node — all day

| | Skylit | Polaris | pairs checked |
|---|---|---|---|
| SPY 740 | **positive** (+7 to +21M) | **negative** (−11 to −44M) | 8 valid pairs, 09:35 → 11:55 |
| QQQ 705 | **negative** (−3 to −47M) | **positive** (+46 to +52M) | 4 valid pairs, 10:42 → 11:27 |

- 100% of time-matched pairs show opposite sign at that one strike. Everything AWAY from
  the king node agrees (e.g. QQQ 702/703/704 matched to the decimal; both put the SPY call
  wall at 747–750 positive).
- It's mirrored across tickers (Skylit +/Polaris − on SPY, Skylit −/Polaris + on QQQ), so
  it's not a one-off data glitch — it's systematic, and it's isolated to how each formula
  signs the big ATM/near-spot gamma node.
- Only moment of agreement: 09:44–09:46 ET, when SPY tagged 744.2 and Skylit's 740 briefly
  went negative too. The divergence appears to be spot-distance dependent.

## Second finding: spot-relative vs strike-anchored magnitudes

As QQQ rallied 705 → 708.9, Skylit's 705 node decayed −47 → −34 → −28 → −22 → −17M.
Polaris held +46–52M the whole time. Skylit's magnitude clearly re-weights by distance from
spot; Polaris is anchored to the strike (consistent with overnight-OI-based math).
`sign-flip-timeline.png` shows both effects in one look.

## Also observed (context, not complaints)

- The SPY 748 wall grew to **$1.5B on Skylit** and pinned the index all afternoon — and it
  was 0DTE: it vanished at the bell when the front expiry rolled. The flip finding is
  expiry-specific (on the new 7/22 front, QQQ 705 read positive on Skylit too).
- Polaris freshness: the "Live" badge is a connection ping, not data freshness — the
  UPDATED stamp is the truth (we caught "Live 22s ago" with 9-min-old data). Rows were
  only logged as valid when UPDATED was actually fresh.

## The three questions

1. **Why does Polaris sign the dominant near-spot node opposite to Skylit?** Most likely
   suspects: dealer long/short assumption at ATM, or put/call gamma sign convention.
2. **What's the sign convention on put gamma below spot?** Skylit paints the below-spot
   floor long-gamma/green; Polaris shows a short pocket (SPY 738–742 negative all day).
   They disagree specifically on what happens on a flush through the floor.
3. **Is Polaris's node magnitude meant to be strike-anchored?** If it's pure OI × gamma at
   the strike, that explains the steadiness — and means Skylit is blending in something
   spot/flow-relative. Knowing which is intentional decides how to read the two together.
